Blood sugar ranges used to define prediabetes

A risk score of 5 or higher means several common risk factors are present[3]. Only a blood test can show whether blood sugar is in the prediabetes range:

Ranges follow the American Diabetes Association[1]. Some bodies, including the WHO, set the lower limit of impaired fasting glucose at 110 mg/dL (6.1 mmol/L). A1C can be unreliable in anaemia or haemoglobin variants. In the absence of clear symptoms, an abnormal result needs confirmation by a repeat test, so please talk to a doctor.

Prevention evidence: in the Diabetes Prevention Program (3,234 adults with prediabetes), intensive lifestyle change (about 7% weight loss and 150 minutes of activity a week) reduced progression to type 2 diabetes by 58%, compared with 31% for metformin[2]. About 8 in 10 US adults with prediabetes do not know they have it[4].
